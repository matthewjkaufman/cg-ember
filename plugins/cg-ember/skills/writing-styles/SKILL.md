# Writing styles

This skill learns how the person writes, one kind of message at a time, and keeps a short
description of each on a page called **My writing styles** in their Personal Notes. The
write-as-me skill reads that page before every draft.

Read the camp-background skill too, for the ground rules.

Emails, messages, invitations and posts are drafted, never sent, as camp-background section 1 says.

## Step 1: which kinds of message

Ask, in one message:

> Which kinds of messages do you write most? For example: replies to parents, notes to
> staff, updates to owners or a board, emails to vendors. Pick as many as you like.

If they already have a **My writing styles** page, read it first and say which kinds it
already covers. They may want to update one, add one, or both.

## Step 2: ask before reading, for each kind

For each kind, one at a time, ask before reading anything:

> Can I read about five emails you've sent recently that are replies to parents? I'll
> keep only a description of how you write, never what you said or who it was to.

Anything other than a clear yes is a no for that kind. Move to the next kind.

## Step 3: read, within the yes

On a yes, read about five of their **sent** emails of that kind, the most recent ones that
are clearly that kind from who they went to and the subject.

- Only sent emails. Never their inbox.
- Only that kind. If you are not sure an email is that kind, skip it.
- Only what they wrote. Skip the earlier messages quoted below their words.
- Skip any email about a camper's health, behavior or safety, or a family's private
  situation.

If fewer than three fit, say so and offer the other way in Step 6.

## Step 4: write the style

Write a short style for that kind, about eight lines:

- **Length**: how long these usually run.
- **Opening**: how they start (a greeting, straight to the point, first names).
- **Closing and sign-off**: how they end and how they sign.
- **Formality**: how relaxed or careful they sound.
- **Contractions**: whether they use them.
- **Habits**: anything they do often, like thanking people first or numbering steps.
- **Words they never use**: ones they name when they see the style, or ones they clearly
  avoid where most people would use them.

**Store style, never content.** No sentence copied from an email. No names of anyone they
wrote to. No detail about any family, camper or staff member. If an example line would
help, invent one with made-up names.

## Step 5: show it, change it, save it

First say in one sentence what you read: "I read five of your replies to parents from the
last month." Then show the style and ask whether any line is wrong. Change any line they
want, in their words.

Then save it on the **My writing styles** page in their Personal Notes, found the way
camp-background section 4 says, under a heading for that kind with today's date. Read the
page before saving and change only that kind's section. If they have no Personal Notes
yet, offer "set me up" once, and leave the style here in the conversation.

Then go on to the next kind they named.

## Step 6: when email is not connected, or they would rather not

They can paste a few examples instead. Ask for three to five of one kind, written by them.
Treat what they paste exactly like Step 3 and Step 4: describe the style, keep nothing
they wrote, and never keep a name.

## Running it again

They can rerun this for one kind at any time ("update my writing style for vendor
emails"). Ask the same permission question for that kind, read again, and replace only
that kind's section. Preferences they gave by hand under "How I like things" in About me
win over anything read from their email.
