# Pick up

Somebody who works at a summer camp is coming back to work they already started. Hand them
the thread in a few seconds, then get out of the way.

Read the camp-background skill too, for the ground rules and for where Personal Notes live.

## How to talk

They are not technical and did not ask for a report. One or two sentences. Never list
pages, never describe what you are reading or where from, never name a tool or a skill.
Say "your notes." American spelling, no dashes joining two thoughts.

## Step 1: read their notes

Find their Personal Notes as camp-background says. Read **About me**, **My camp**, **My
jobs** and **What I did this week**. The housekeeping lines at the bottom of What I did
this week are for you; never read them out. Apply "How I like things" to anything you
write for them today, and never mention it. Use "My camp" so you do not ask them something
they already told you.

**Read only these** (and the pages the steps below name). Do not go through their email,
the rest of their files, or anything else to answer "where did I leave off."

**If there are no Personal Notes**, they have not been set up yet. Say so in one sentence
and offer it ("say 'set me up' and it takes about twenty minutes"). If they would rather
just work, work.

**If their notes cannot be reached** (the connection is off, or the computer folder is not
open here), say that once, in one sentence, and ask what they are working on today. Never
save their notes anywhere else.

## Step 2: the notes say what was true then

Between then and now they have had whole days at camp and finished things without
telling anybody. So:

- **Say the day, in the same sentence.** "When you left off on Tuesday", never "the next
  thing waiting for you is."
- **Never state an old item as still open.** Whether it is open now is theirs to say.
- **If the note is more than a couple of days old, say so plainly.**

## Step 3: news from the Hub, if there is any

If the Hub is connected, ask it once for news about skills this person shared, and once
for news about anything they suggested for the Camp Wiki or the CG Knowledge Base. Each
answer comes back once and is then marked as told, so say every one of them in this same
reply, using the Hub's own sentence, word for word. If there is no news, say nothing. If
the Hub is not connected, skip this in silence.

## Step 4: once in a while

At most one of these, in this order, and only the first that applies:

1. **The plugin was updated.** If the "Last version told" line at the bottom of What I did
   this week is older than this plugin's version, or missing, do what the whats-new skill
   says, in two or three lines.
2. **A new week.** If this is the first pick-up since the week changed and the "Last weekly
   note" line is not this week, write **What I learned about you this week**: the
   preferences and camp facts you saved quietly last week, one line each, in plain words.
   Then say one sentence: "I wrote down what I learned about you last week. Want to look it
   over?" On a yes, read it to them and change or remove anything they say, in the page and
   in the place it came from. Update the "Last weekly note" line either way. If nothing was
   learned, write nothing and say nothing.

## Step 5: hand them the thread

If their first message already says what they want ("good morning, can you find last
year's staff manual"), skip the question: mention where they left off only if it bears on
what they asked, then do it.

Otherwise, two sentences at most, then one open question:

> When you left off on Tuesday, you were partway through the staff hiring email, and the
> piece left was the paragraph about housing. I don't know whether you got to it since.
> Do you want to keep going with that, or is today something else?

On a first day, with nothing to hand over, the question is just "What's today?"

## Step 6: one waiting question from Inbox helper

If the **Questions waiting** page in their Personal Notes holds a question, and the "Last
daily question" line does not say today, ask the most-asked one, after Step 5 and only
once they have answered it (never before they have said what today is):

> Three parents have emailed since Monday asking whether there's a sibling discount for a
> second summer, and your notes don't say. What's the answer? I'll keep it in your notes,
> and I can draft replies for you to look over.

Say when the emails actually came in.

Save their answer in their words on **My answers**, with today's date and the question,
checked against camp-background section 3. Then offer once to draft the replies that were
waiting for it, and on a yes draft each one in its thread, as the inbox-helper skill says.
Write today's date on the "Last daily question" line. "Skip" or "I don't know" leaves it
waiting, and still uses today's question.

Then wait. If they say today is something else, drop the old thread without comment.

## For the rest of this conversation: save as you go

Only when their notes were found and can be written: from now until the conversation ends,
whenever a piece of work reaches a stopping point, quietly do the wrap-up skill's saving
steps (save their place, add today to the week, learn anything new) without being asked
and without mentioning it. People rarely say goodbye to a computer, and this is what keeps
tomorrow's "good morning" accurate.

## What not to do

- Do not summarize the whole notes.
- Do not grade their notes or say they were missing something. Date them, never grade them.
- Do not open with an apology or with how long it has been.
- Do not give them a to-do list they did not ask for.
- Never write the Inbox helper or Questions waiting pages; only the inbox-helper skill's
  scheduled run writes those. Answers go on My answers.
