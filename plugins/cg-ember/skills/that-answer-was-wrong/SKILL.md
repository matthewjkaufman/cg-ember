# That answer was wrong

Somebody who works at a summer camp just got an answer that was wrong or out of date.
Fix it where it came from, so neither they nor anyone else gets it again.

Read the camp-background skill too, for the ground rules.

Emails, messages, invitations and posts are drafted, never sent, as camp-background section 1 says.

## Step 1: which answer, and where it came from

The answer is usually the last one in this conversation. If it is not clear which answer
they mean, ask once ("The bus pickup time, or the swim test day?").

Then work out where it came from. Answers name their source (camp-background section 4),
so look at what was said:

- **Their Personal Notes**: go to Step 2.
- **A Camp Wiki or CG Knowledge Base page**: go to Step 3.
- **One of their own files** (an email, a document): go to Step 4.
- **No source**, because it came from you and not from anything of theirs: say so in
  one sentence, ask what the right answer is, and offer to keep it in "My answers" as in
  Step 4.

## Step 2: their Personal Notes, fixed now

Their notes are theirs, so this needs no permission.

1. If they did not say what is right, ask once: "What's the right answer?"
2. Read the page first. Change only the wrong part. Leave everything else as it was.
3. Say it in one sentence: "Fixed. Your notes now say the pool opens June 22."

Two pages are not theirs to change by hand: **Inbox helper** and **Questions waiting**,
which only Inbox helper's scheduled run writes. If the wrong answer is on one of those,
leave the page alone and put the right answer in **My answers** instead. The next run
reads it from there.

## Step 3: a Camp Wiki or CG Knowledge Base page

**If the Hub is connected here:**

1. Ask one question, unless they already said both: "What's wrong with it, and do you
   know the right answer?" Not knowing the right answer is fine.
2. Write the report: what is wrong, and the right answer if they gave one. Check it
   against camp-background section 3 first: no camper's last name, no parent's name, no
   medical or private family details, nothing about how a staff member is doing.
3. Show them the exact words and ask: "Want me to report this to whoever looks after that
   page?" Anything other than a clear yes is a no.
4. On a yes, report it to that page's approver through the Hub. Say the Hub's sentence
   back word for word, and add nothing to it.
5. Whatever they said in step 3, save a line in **My answers** in their Personal Notes,
   so the wrong answer is not repeated while it waits:

   ```
   The Camp Wiki page on swim tests says Tuesday; the right answer is Wednesday,
   reported on October 20.
   ```

   If they did not report it, end the line with "not reported" instead of the date. If
   they did not know the right answer, write "the right answer is not known yet".

If the Hub refuses the report, say its sentence word for word, and still save the line
in My answers. If nothing comes back or something breaks, follow camp-background
section 6, and still save the line.

**If the Hub is not connected here**, save the line in **My answers** with "not reported"
at the end, and say plainly in one sentence: "I've kept the right answer in your notes,
but I couldn't tell the Camp Wiki from here."

## Step 4: one of their own files

Their files belong to them, so never change one without a clear yes.

1. Say which file it came from, in words ("your email from the bus company on
   September 28", "the document called Staff Handbook 2026").
2. Ask what is right, if they did not say.
3. Offer, in one sentence, to keep the right answer in **My answers** so it is used next
   time. On a yes, save a line there: "The Staff Handbook 2026 says check-in is at 8; the
   right answer is 8:30, noted on October 20."
4. Only if they ask, help them change the file itself, and show the change before making
   it.

## Ending

One sentence that says what changed and where. Then stop. No apology speech, and no
explanation of how answers are found unless they ask.

Camper and family details never go into My answers or a report (camp-background
section 3). If the wrong answer was about one child, write the fix about the rule or the
document, never the child.
