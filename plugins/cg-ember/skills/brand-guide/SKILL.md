# Build our brand guide

A camp's brand is how it looks and sounds everywhere: the logo, the colors, the fonts, the
photos, and the words. When it is written down, every flyer, sign, handout and email I help
with uses the same colors and fonts and sounds like the same camp. When it is not, each one
comes out a little different.

This skill writes the camp's brand guide with the person, by asking short questions one at
a time, and by reading what they already have, only when they say yes. Usually the person is
a director, or whoever looks after how the camp looks.

Read the camp-background skill too, for the ground rules.

Emails, messages, invitations and posts are drafted, never sent, as camp-background section 1 says.

## The one rule that never bends

**Never invent a color code, a font, or a rule.** A color code is written down only when it
appears as text in something you read (their brand file, their website's own code) or when
the person types it. A font is written down only when it is named the same way. Never guess
a code from how a color looks in a picture or on screen, and never name a font because a
logo looks like it. Anything nobody knows yet stays in the guide as **Not known yet**, with
one line on where to find it (often the designer who made the logo, or the printer who
prints the shirts). A guide with three honest gaps is useful. A guide with one made-up code
puts the wrong blue on four hundred shirts.

## How to talk

- One question at a time. Wait for the answer before the next one.
- Short sentences. Under each question, offer an example so they can answer quickly.
- Define a word the first time you use it, in the same sentence. The one most people meet
  here: a **color code** is a # followed by six letters and numbers, like #1F4E79, that
  tells every program the exact shade. Printers sometimes use other codes (a Pantone number,
  or four CMYK numbers); write those down too when they have them.
- The questions here do not count against the one question a day that several skills
  share. The person asked for this.

## Step 1: say why, then see whether there is one already

Open with one sentence that says why this is worth doing:

> Once this is written down, every flyer and email I help with will use your exact colors
> and words.

Then look, and say nothing about looking:

- the **Our brand** section of the **My camp** page in their Personal Notes, found the way
  camp-background section 4 says;
- a brand guide page in the Camp Wiki, when the Hub is connected.

If one exists, say so in one sentence, with its date, and ask whether they want to update
part of it or go through all of it. Then ask only about the parts they name.

## Step 2: what they already have, read only with a yes

Ask each of these on its own, and move on after anything other than a clear yes.

1. **A brand file.** "Do you already have anything written about your brand, like a PDF
   from a designer or a Doc with your colors and fonts? If so, where is it?" On a yes, read
   the one they point to. Take the codes and font names exactly as written there.
2. **Your website.** "Can I read a few pages of your camp's public website, to pick up the
   colors, fonts and words you already use? I'll read only your home page and the pages you
   name. The exact color codes usually sit in the site's style files, which are part of the
   same public website; may I read those too?" On a yes, read only those pages, and the
   style files only if they said yes to them. A color code or font name counts only when it
   is written in the page's own text or code, and a website often carries leftovers from
   the company that built it, so read back each one you found and ask which are really the
   camp's.
3. **A few family emails.** "Want me to read two or three emails you sent to families that
   sound most like your camp? You pick them. I'll keep only how they sound and the words
   you use, never what they said or who they went to." On a yes, read only the ones they
   pick. Skip any about a camper's health, behavior or safety, or a family's private
   situation. Keep words and tone, never a sentence about a family, and never a name.

Say in one sentence what you read and what you found, before asking about the gaps.

**Reading colors back.** Never show a bare list of codes. Name each color in plain words,
say where it appeared, and put the code after, one at a time: "A dark navy blue on your
buttons (#1F4E79). Is it yours?" Do not try to show a colored square; a chat cannot draw
one reliably.

## Step 3: fill the gaps, one question at a time

Ask only about what is still missing, in this order. `reference/brand-guide-template.md`
in this skill's folder holds the guide's shape.

1. **The name.** How the camp's name is written in full, the short form people use, and any
   way it is never written. (For example: "Pine Hollow Day Camp. Short: Pine Hollow. Never
   PHDC.")
2. **The logo.** Which versions exist (full color, one color, white for dark backgrounds),
   what each file is called and where it lives, in words ("the Logos folder in our shared
   Drive"). Any rules: space around it, never stretched, never on a busy photo. Never make
   or change a logo, and never ask them to paste one in.
3. **Colors.** Each color's name, its code, and what it is for (headings, buttons, the
   background). Ask for the code in their words: "Do you know the code for your green? It
   starts with a #." If they do not, it stays **Not known yet**.
4. **Fonts.** The font for headings and the font for regular text, by name. Then: "When you
   don't have that font, like in an email, what do you use instead?" If they do not know, it
   stays **Not known yet**. Never choose one for them.
5. **Photos.** What a good camp photo shows, and any rules. Ask: "Are there rules about
   which campers can appear in photos, or how?" Write the rule they give, in general words.
   Never write a camper's name, and never list campers whose families said no.
6. **Words.** Words the camp uses ("bunk", "counselor", "families"), and words it avoids
   (for example "daycare", "cheap", "drop-off"). How it refers to campers, staff and parents.
7. **Voice.** Three words for how the camp sounds ("warm, upbeat, plain"). Then a short
   sample, three or four sentences, in the camp's voice: one they wrote and like, or one
   you write together from what they told you, which they approve word for word. No family,
   camper or staff names in it.

If they want to stop partway, keep what you have, mark the rest **Not known yet**, and go to
Step 4. Saying "build our brand guide" again picks up the gaps.

## Step 4: show the whole guide before saving

Show the finished guide, in full, in the template's shape. Then ask: "Is anything here
wrong or missing?" Change what they say, in their words, and show the changed part again.
Save nothing until they say it is right.

## Step 5: save it where the next job will find it

**When the Hub is connected**, offer to suggest it for the Camp Wiki:

> Want me to suggest this for your Camp Wiki, the pages everyone at your camp can read? The
> person at your camp who looks after those pages decides whether it goes in, and your name
> goes with it.

Anything other than a clear yes is a no. On a yes, suggest it as one page called "Brand
guide", exactly as they approved it, checked against camp-background section 3, and say the
Hub's answer word for word. Then offer once to keep a copy in their Personal Notes as well,
so their own drafts use it while the approver decides.

**When the two copies disagree**, the Camp Wiki copy wins once the approver has put it in,
because the camp approved it. Until then, the Personal Notes copy is used. When you notice
they differ, say so in one sentence and offer once to bring the Personal Notes copy in line
with the wiki.

**When the Hub is not connected**, or they would rather keep it to themselves, save it on
their **My camp** page in their Personal Notes, under a heading **Our brand** with today's
date. Read the page first and change only that section. If they have no Personal Notes yet,
ask once:

> Want me to set up your Personal Notes first?

On a yes, run the set-me-up skill's Personal Notes step, then save it. Otherwise leave the
guide here in the conversation.

If they ask for a copy to share with a designer or a printer, make it a document in their
files and say which folder in one sentence. Never share it with anyone yourself.

Then one sentence on what changes: "From now on, when I make a flyer, a handout or a draft
for camp, I'll use these colors, fonts and words."

## How other skills use it

Any job that makes something with the camp's name on it (write-as-me, check-its-clear,
Inbox helper's drafts, or a flyer, a sign or a slide) looks for **Our brand** in My camp, or
the Camp Wiki's brand guide, and uses its words, colors and fonts. Where the guide says
**Not known yet**, that job leaves the choice to the person and never fills it in.

## What this does not do

- It never invents a color code, a font, a rule or a voice sample.
- It never reads their website, a file or an email without a yes for that one thing.
- It never makes, changes or uploads a logo, and never changes their website.
- It never puts a camper's or a family's name or details in the guide.
- It never sends the guide to anyone.
