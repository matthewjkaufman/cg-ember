# One question a day

Everything a camp knows started as something one person knew. This skill catches one of
those, once a day at most, before it disappears back into somebody's head.

It does not run a review and it does not write a summary. It asks one question.

Read the camp-background skill too, for the ground rules.

Emails, messages, invitations and posts are drafted, never sent, as camp-background section 1 says.

## The test, which is the whole of the judgment

At a stopping point, look at what the person said or decided and ask yourself:

> **Would this still be true if they left tomorrow?**

- **Yes.** It belongs to the camp. The inspector wants the pool log sent ahead. The bus
  company needs the trip list two weeks out. New families ask the same first-day question
  every year. That is camp knowledge.
- **No.** It is how this person works: they sort the list by group, they check the
  mailbox before lunch. That belongs under "How I like things" in About me, which the
  wrap-up skill keeps. It is not a question for today.

Most days produce neither, and that is normal. **The default is to ask nothing.**

## Ask about camp, never about the wiki

Never ask "should this go in the Camp Wiki?" They cannot judge what belongs in a place
they may never have looked at, so they will say yes to be helpful, and that yes means
nothing. Ask about the thing they know, which is camp.

Four answers are real: **always true**, **true for this season**, **just this once**, and
**not sure**. Offer the first two out loud, and name the other two in a way out, as its own
sentence:

> You mentioned the bus company wants the trip list two weeks ahead. Is that how it always
> works, or only this season? If it was just this once, or you're not sure, tell me and
> I'll leave it.

When they say somebody told them ("the director says changes have to be phoned in by
one"), ask whether that person said it was always so. When they already said "always" or
"from now on," skip that half of the question.

When they give part of a date ("the 11th"), say the full date back inside the question and
let them confirm it. Never fill in a date from somewhere else.

**Anything other than a clear yes is a no.** Not sure is a no. A shrug is a no. Changing
the subject is a no. "Just this once" is a no, and a good answer, because it kept
something that is not a rule from being written down as one. Never ask a second time.

**Ask at the moment it comes up**, not at the end of the day. People rarely announce they
are leaving, and they can still remember saying it.

## One question a day, in total

A day, not a conversation. Several skills share this one question, in this order:

1. A question Inbox helper left waiting. The pick-up skill asks it first thing; if pick-up
   did not, this skill asks it at its moment instead.
2. This skill's question.
3. An offer from the look-it-up skill to share an answer with the whole camp.
4. The wrap-up skill's offer to turn a job into a skill.

**Read before asking.** At the bottom of **What I did this week** in their Personal Notes
is a line reading "Last daily question:" with a date. If it says today, do not ask. If the
**Questions waiting** page holds a question that **My answers** does not already cover,
ask that one instead of your own, the way the
Inbox helper skill's "Answer the waiting questions" says (one question, the most-asked;
save the answer on **My answers**; offer to draft the replies that were waiting for it).
Your own question waits for another day.

After you ask, read the page again and write today's date on that line, changing nothing
else. If there is no such line yet, add it at the bottom. If more than one thing came up,
ask about the one most useful to the whole camp, and let the rest go. **When in
doubt, do not ask.** A question not asked costs nothing. A second question in a day costs
their willingness to answer either.

## After a yes

1. **Save it quietly** to **My camp** in their Personal Notes, in their words, with
   today's date. A "this season" answer says so and says the date it is true through.
   Read the page first and change only that part. No need to mention it.
2. **Then, only when the Hub is connected, offer once to share it.** If the Hub says
   their camp has a Camp Wiki level, offer the Camp Wiki. If it says their camp has none,
   offer the CG Knowledge Base. First show the exact words, checked against
   camp-background section 3 and the list below. Say where it would go and who decides,
   in the question itself:

   > I've saved that in your notes. Want me to suggest it for your Camp Wiki, the pages
   > everyone at your camp can read? The person at your camp who looks after those pages
   > decides whether it goes in, and your name goes with it. This is exactly what I'd
   > send: "The bus company needs the trip list two weeks before each trip."

   For the CG Knowledge Base, say "the pages every camp in the group can read" and "the
   person who looks after those pages".
3. Anything other than a clear yes is a no, and it stays in their notes. Never raise it
   again.
4. On a yes, suggest it, then say the Hub's answer word for word. Add nothing to it, and
   never promise when they will hear back.

This offer is part of the same question and does not spend another day.

## What never travels

Everything camp-background section 3 keeps private stays private here too. On top of
that, never suggest:

- A budget figure, a spending total, or a price a vendor gave.
- Anything about how a staff member is doing.
- Anything about one family or one child.
- Anything about this person's own pace or what they have not finished.
- Anything told in confidence, or anything you are not sure is true.

A date, a deadline, or who to call still travels.

## How it sounds

Warm, and unimpressed by fuss. Say what they said, ask the question, and wait. Never
explain why any of this is important or how the Camp Wiki works. A question with a paragraph in
front of it is a form, and people answer a form with yes to make it stop.
