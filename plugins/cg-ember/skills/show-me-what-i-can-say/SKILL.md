# Show me what I can say

Somebody who works at a summer camp wants to know what they can ask for. Give them the
phrases, short and grouped, so they can find the one they need at a glance.

Read the camp-background skill too, for the ground rules.

Emails, messages, invitations and posts are drafted, never sent, as camp-background section 1 says.

## Before you write the list

Check two things quietly, and say nothing about checking:

- **Whether their Personal Notes exist**, the way camp-background section 4 says to find
  them.
- **Whether the Hub is connected here** (camp-background section 4).

## The list

Five short groups, in this order. Each line is the phrase in bold, then what happens in a
few words. Never show a folder name, a skill's hyphenated name, or how anything works
underneath. Say each phrase the way a person would say it out loud.

**Getting started**

- **"Set me up"**: connects your email, files and calendar, and makes your Personal Notes.
- **"Make my own copy of wrap up"** (or any of these): a version you can change, safe from updates.
- **"Show me what I can say"**: this list.
- **"What's new"**: what changed since the last update.

**Every day**

- **"Good morning"** or **"pick up where I left off"**: where you stopped last time.
- **"Wrap up"**: saves your place for tomorrow.
- **"Prep me for my meeting"**: a one-screen brief before you walk in.
- **"Set up Inbox helper"**: every few hours, drafts replies to emails your notes can
  answer and lists the rest. It never sends.
- Nothing to say for **one question a day**: once a day at most, I ask one short question
  about how your camp works, so it is written down.

**Writing**

- **"Write as me"**: a draft in your own voice, waiting in your drafts.
- **"Build my writing styles"**: learns how you write each kind of message.
- **"Is this clear?"** or **"will they understand this?"**: reads it as the person
  receiving it would.
- **"Explain that"**: anything about AI, or about me, in plain words.

**Camp knowledge**

- **"Look it up"**, or just ask, like **"what's our policy on..."** or **"when is..."**:
  answers from your Personal Notes, the Camp Wiki and the CG Knowledge Base, and says
  where each answer came from.
- **"That answer was wrong"**: fixes it in your notes and reports it to whoever looks
  after that page.

**Sharing**

- **"Share this skill"**: shares a skill you built, for review, so other camps can use it.

## When the Hub is not connected

Change two lines, and leave out nothing:

- **"Look it up"**: answers from your Personal Notes and your files; from the Camp Wiki
  and the CG Knowledge Base once your camp is on the Hub.
- **"That answer was wrong"**: fixes it in your notes; reports it to the Camp Wiki and the
  CG Knowledge Base once your camp is on the Hub.

Do not explain what the Hub is unless they ask. If they ask, one sentence: the Hub is the
website that holds the Camp Wiki and the CG Knowledge Base.

## End with one thing to try

One sentence, after the list, picked from what you found:

- **No Personal Notes yet**: "Start with 'set me up'. It takes about twenty minutes."
- **Personal Notes, but no "My writing styles" page**: "A good first one: 'build my
  writing styles', so drafts sound like you."
- **Writing styles, but no "Inbox helper" page**: "A good next one: 'set up Inbox
  helper'."
- **All of those**: "Try 'prep me for my meeting' before your next one."

Then stop. No closing paragraph, and no offer to explain any of them unless they ask.

## If they ask about one line

Answer in two or three sentences: what to say, what happens, and what they get at the
end. If they want to start it, start it.
